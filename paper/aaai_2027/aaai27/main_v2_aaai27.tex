\begin{abstract}
Continuous-time tangent-projection safety filters (CBF, ATACOM) guarantee forward invariance under infinitesimal time steps, but at coarse control rates ($\Delta t{=}0.1$\,s) the agent's carried momentum overshoots a position-only constraint and \textbf{every published filter we test fails} the GO threshold ($\bar C\!\le\!5$) on at least one of three Safety-Gymnasium tasks. We trace this to a single cause --- the obstacle constraint ignores momentum --- and fix it by augmenting the barrier with the braking distance: $c(\boldsymbol q,\boldsymbol v){=}\rho(\boldsymbol q){-}r_\text{base}{-}\|\boldsymbol v\|^2{/}(2a_{\max}){\ge}0$. The proposed \textbf{VA-ATACOM} filter projects the policy action onto the tangent cone of this velocity-augmented constraint manifold. Our central result is \textbf{Proposition~4}: forward invariance under explicit, constructive conditions in $(\Delta t, r, v_{\max})$ --- the \emph{first discrete-time analogue} of ATACOM's continuous-time invariance theorem, reducing to it as $\Delta t\to0$ and recovering the widely-used velocity-adaptive margin as its first-order approximation. On Safety-Gymnasium under fair hazard-radius matching, VA-ATACOM attains 0.00 / 0.00 / 0.40 mean episode cost (\textbf{15/15 GO --- the only method to do so}), versus the best published filter (HOCBF 4/15) and the velocity-margin heuristic it subsumes (13/15). A 240-trial Webots E-puck study confirms transfer to a coarse-rate ($\Delta t{=}64$\,ms) rigid-body simulator with neither filter nor policy retuning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%==============================================================================

We present \textbf{VA-ATACOM} (Velocity-Augmented ATACOM), the first \emph{tangent-projection} safety filter on a velocity-augmented constraint manifold with an explicit discrete-time forward-invariance theorem. VA-ATACOM closes a two-orders-of-magnitude gap between the continuous-time theory of tangent-projection filters (ATACOM \cite{liu2022,liu2024,liu2025}, CBF \cite{ames2014,ames2017}, and their variants) and the $\Delta t = 0.1$s reality of Safe-RL benchmarks \cite{ji2023}, where every published filter we tested fails.

\textbf{Why VA-ATACOM is needed.} Continuous-time tangent-projection filters guarantee forward invariance via the ODE $\dot{\boldsymbol{q}} = \boldsymbol{N}_c\boldsymbol{\alpha} - K_c\boldsymbol{J}_c^{+}\boldsymbol{c}$, but discretisation at $\Delta t = 0.1$s breaks the guarantee. We benchmark eight published tangent-projection filters on three Safety-Gymnasium tasks (Goal, Push, MultiGoal) under 5 seeds $\times$ 200K env steps with each filter's keepout matched to the env's true hazard radius: \textbf{every published method fails the GO threshold ($\bar{C} \le 5$) on at least one task; their best total reaches 4/15 cells (HOCBF).} A PPO baseline with no filter achieves 2/15. Two structural mechanisms drive these failures, which VA-ATACOM is designed to address:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{M1 (chord excursion and bearing rotation).} Even under exact tangent projection at step $k$, Pythagoras gives $\rho_{k+1}^2 = \rho_k^2 + \Delta t^2 \|u\|^2$: the discrete chord overshoots the continuous arc, and the obstacle's bearing rotates by $\phi \approx \Delta t\|u\|/\rho$. A committed world-frame velocity acquires inward radial component $\approx -\Delta t\|u\|^2/\rho$ at the next step. The effect is operationally significant when $\Delta t \|u\| \gtrsim \sqrt{d_\text{safe} \cdot r}$; at $\Delta t = 0.1$s, PPO-learned $\|u\| \approx 1$m/s, $r = 0.2$m, the threshold is reached.

\item \textbf{M2 (sustained-commitment drift).} The rotation in M1 compounds: under $n$ steps of sustained commitment, the cumulative inward drift is $\Delta\rho_n \sim n\Delta t^2 \|u\|^2/\rho$ (linear in $n$). On Safety-Gym, $n^\star = 2$ steps suffices to exhaust the safety margin, so no single-step ($h{=}1$) projector can recover once the agent commits to a tangent trajectory.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The fix: a velocity-augmented constraint manifold.} Both failure modes stem from momentum: at coarse $\Delta t$ the agent carries velocity that a static keepout constraint $\rho \ge r_\text{base}$ does not account for. VA-ATACOM replaces it with $c(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{v}) = \rho - r_\text{base} - \|\boldsymbol{v}\|^2/(2a_{\max}) \ge 0$, whose zero-level set is a manifold in $(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{v})$ state space: the agent must keep enough room to brake. The policy action is projected onto this manifold's tangent cone (null-space projection in the diff-drive action frame). The linear velocity-adaptive margin $r_\text{base}(1+\alpha\|\boldsymbol{v}\|)$ used by prior heuristics is its \emph{first-order} approximation. A backward-reachable-tube (BRT) reward-shaping term --- a short worst-case rollout --- additionally speeds training on the M2 goal-switching transients, but is an auxiliary training aid, not part of the safety guarantee.

\textbf{Theoretical contribution: Proposition 4.} We prove that VA-ATACOM yields positive invariance of the inflated keepout set under the explicit \emph{rule-of-thumb} conditions
\begin{equation}
\alpha \;\gtrsim\; \frac{\Delta t^2 v_{\max}}{r}
\quad\text{and}\quad
h \;\ge\; \left\lceil \frac{r}{\Delta t \cdot v_{\max}} \right\rceil,
\end{equation}
which we sharpen in \S\ref{sec:prop4} to a rigorous sufficient condition involving a danger-zone buffer $d_\text{danger}$. This is the \textbf{first discrete-time analogue} of the ATACOM continuous-time forward-invariance theorem: as $\Delta t \to 0$, VA-ATACOM reduces to ATACOM (Corollary 1), and at coarse $\Delta t$ the conditions are constructive — given $\Delta t$, $r$, and $v_{\max}$, they prescribe the filter's hyperparameters.

\textbf{Experimental validation.} On Safety-Gymnasium with each filter's keepout matched to the env's hazard radius, VA-ATACOM achieves \textbf{near-zero cost on all three tasks} --- 0.00 Goal, 0.00 Push, 0.40 MultiGoal; 15/15 GO --- versus 0.84 / 3.36 / 2.59 for the strongest prior filter (our heuristic DT-margin) and at least one NO-GO task for every published filter. A five-design study (\S\ref{sec:design-study}) shows the velocity augmentation is what makes the constraint-manifold projection succeed: removing it (null-space ATACOM) or replacing projection with model-free scaling both fail.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Velocity-augmented constraint manifold and its discrete-time invariance theorem} (\S\ref{sec:method}, \S\ref{sec:prop4}): we augment the obstacle barrier with the agent's braking distance and prove \textbf{Proposition 4} --- forward invariance of the resulting manifold under explicit, constructive conditions. This is the \emph{first discrete-time analogue} of ATACOM's continuous-time invariance theorem, with \textbf{Corollary~1} (it reduces to ATACOM as $\Delta t\to0$) and \textbf{Corollary~2} (multi-obstacle case at $O(m)$ per step via $\text{rhs}_{\min}{=}\min_i \text{rhs}_i$). It recovers the widely-used velocity-adaptive margin as its first-order approximation, explaining why that heuristic worked.

\item \textbf{Failure-mode diagnosis} (\S\ref{sec:failure}): Propositions 1--2 (M1 chord excursion, M2 drift) pinpoint \emph{why} every continuous-time tangent-projection filter loses safety at coarse $\Delta t$ --- the carried momentum that a static constraint ignores.

\item \textbf{Five-design study} (\S\ref{sec:design-study}): across null-space ATACOM, discrete CBF-QP, model-free braking scaling, and static- vs.\ velocity-augmented projection, we isolate \emph{velocity augmentation together with tangent-space projection} as jointly necessary --- scaling alone or a static manifold both fail.

\item \textbf{Empirical validation across simulation and sim-to-real} (\S\ref{sec:experiments}): VA-ATACOM is \emph{the only method} reaching \textbf{15/15 GO} on Safety-Gymnasium (0.00 / 0.00 / 0.40 mean cost on Goal / Push / MultiGoal), with paired Wilcoxon $p\!<\!0.001$ against every baseline. A 240-trial Webots E-puck study (corridor, dense field, and L-shape barrier with a 154\,mm tightest-corner gap; both a P-controller and a Safety-Gym-trained PPO policy) confirms physical-units transfer: \textbf{1/120 vs 24/120} deep penetrations with vs without the filter (paired McNemar $p{=}1.2{\times}10^{-7}$), the filter never causing a regression.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
%==============================================================================

A recent survey \cite{brunke2022} organises safe learning for control along three structurally distinct directions; VA-ATACOM belongs to the first family but addresses a gap that the others do not fill.

\subsection{Tangent-Projection Safety Filters}

A safety filter intercepts the policy's commanded action and projects it onto the constraint manifold's tangent space at each control instant. Forward invariance is proven in continuous time by zeroing the radial component of the constraint Jacobian.

Representative works include null-space projection \cite{khatib1987,sentis2005}, the ATACOM family \cite{liu2022,liu2024,liu2025}, control barrier functions \cite{ames2014,ames2017}, higher-order CBF \cite{xiao2021}, and discrete-time CBF (DCM) \cite{agrawal2017}. Mobile-robot CBF instantiations \cite{cheng2019,taylor2020}, hybrid HJ$+$CBF schemes \cite{choi2020}, and learned barriers \cite{robey2020,lindemann2023} extend the family but inherit the continuous-time invariance argument.

\textbf{The common assumption.} All these methods derive their guarantees from continuous-time analysis (ODEs) or single-step discrete analysis. The original ATACOM paper evaluates on high-frequency manipulator control ($\Delta t \le 2$ms) where the continuous-time assumption holds empirically. When ported to Safety-Gymnasium at $\Delta t = 0.1$s, the assumption is violated by two orders of magnitude.

\textbf{Position vs HOCBF and DCBF.} HOCBF \cite{xiao2021} handles relative-degree-2 systems in continuous time with empirically-tuned class-K coefficients; DCBF \cite{agrawal2017} decays the \emph{static} barrier at a constant rate $h_{k+1} \ge (1{-}\gamma)h_k$, defeated by M1 (\S\ref{sec:failure}). VA-ATACOM differs on two axes: the braking augmentation turns DCBF's constant-$\gamma$ into a \emph{state-dependent} rate, and Prop.~4 supplies a closed-form discretisation defect $\delta{=}\tfrac{3}{2}\Delta t^2 a_{\max}$ plus a constructive condition --- $(d_\text{safe},\alpha_0)$ from $(\Delta t, r, v_{\max})$ --- which HOCBF and DCBF leave as hyperparameters. Empirically HOCBF reaches 4/15 GO and DCBF (DCM) 3/15, VA-ATACOM 15/15 (Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\subsection{Set-Based and Predictive Safety Methods}

Set-based methods compute a safe set offline (the backward-reachable tube under worst-case disturbances) and enforce safety at runtime by querying the set. Representative works include Hamilton-Jacobi reachability \cite{mitchell2005,bansal2017}, safety Bellman equations \cite{fisac2018,fisac2019,hsu2021}, predictive safety filters \cite{wabersich2018,wabersich2021}, recovery zones \cite{thananjeyan2021}, and learned safe sets \cite{robey2020}.

VA-ATACOM's multi-step BRT lookahead is a discrete-time, online approximation of the HJ backward-reachable tube. Instead of solving the HJB PDE offline, we forward-simulate 9 worst-case directions over $h$ steps at each filter call.

\subsection{Lagrangian Soft Constraints}

Lagrangian methods relax the hard safety constraint into a penalty term whose multiplier is learned online \cite{achiam2017,ray2019,tessler2018,yang2020,stooke2020}; the OmniSafe framework \cite{ji2024} is now the standard benchmark suite. These methods provide no per-step guarantee; safety is achieved in expectation over the episode. We include PPO-Lag in the benchmark as a soft-constraint reference; it achieves 1/15 GO.

\section{Preliminaries}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Constrained MDP Setting}

We consider safe reinforcement learning in the constrained MDP (CMDP) framework. A CMDP is a tuple $(S, A, P, r, c, \gamma, \bar{C})$ where $c: S \times A \to \{0, 1\}$ is a binary cost indicator and $\bar{C}$ the episode cost threshold.

\textbf{Benchmark.} We evaluate on Safety-Gymnasium \cite{ji2023}, specifically the Point-Robot environment with Goal, Push, and MultiGoal tasks:
\begin{itemize}
\item Time step: $\Delta t = 0.1$s (10 Hz control)
\item Obstacles: 8 circular hazards with radius $r = 0.2$m
\item Cost function: $c = 1$ if agent-hazard distance $< r$, else $c = 0$
\item GO threshold: $\bar{C} \le 5.0$ (mean over 50 evaluation episodes)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{ATACOM Continuous-Time Theorem}

ATACOM \cite{liu2022} provides forward invariance through null-space projection. Given constraint function $\boldsymbol{c}(\boldsymbol{q}): \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ and RL action $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$, ATACOM transforms it via:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\boldsymbol{q}} = \boldsymbol{N}_c \boldsymbol{\alpha} - K_c \boldsymbol{J}_c^+ \boldsymbol{c}(\boldsymbol{q})
\label{eq:atacom}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{N}_c = \boldsymbol{I} - \boldsymbol{J}_c^+ \boldsymbol{J}_c$ is the null-space projector.

\textbf{Theorem 1} (Liu 2022). Under dynamics (\ref{eq:atacom}), the constraint evolves as $\dot{\boldsymbol{c}} = -K_c \boldsymbol{c}$, hence $\boldsymbol{c}(t) = \boldsymbol{c}(0) e^{-K_c t}$. The safe set is forward invariant.

\textbf{Discretisation gap.} When implemented with Euler integration at $\Delta t = 0.1$s: $\boldsymbol{q}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{q}_k + \Delta t \cdot \boldsymbol{u}_k$, the continuous-time guarantee degrades.

\subsection{Circular Obstacle Geometry}

For a circular hazard at $\boldsymbol{p}_o$ with radius $r$, the signed distance constraint is:
\begin{equation}
c(\boldsymbol{q}) = r^2 - \|\boldsymbol{q} - \boldsymbol{p}_o\|^2
\end{equation}
where $c < 0$ means safe, $c = 0$ on boundary, $c > 0$ inside (violation).

Let $\rho = \|\boldsymbol{q} - \boldsymbol{p}_o\|$ and decompose velocity as $\boldsymbol{u} = u_\rho \hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\rho + u_\theta \hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta$. A tangent-projection filter zeroes $u_\rho$ when $c \ge 0$.

\section{VA-ATACOM Algorithm}
\label{sec:method}
%==============================================================================

This section presents VA-ATACOM. We first diagnose why continuous-time filters fail (\S\ref{sec:failure}), then present the solution (\S\ref{sec:design}), and prove discrete-time forward invariance (\S\ref{sec:prop4}).

\subsection{Why Continuous-Time Filters Fail}
\label{sec:failure}

\subsubsection{M1: Chord Excursion and Bearing Rotation}

\textbf{Proposition 1} (Single-step bearing rotation). Let $\rho_k := \|\boldsymbol{q}_k - \boldsymbol{p}_o\| > 0$ and decompose the velocity as $\boldsymbol{u}_k = u_\rho \hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\rho^{(k)} + u_\theta \hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta^{(k)}$ with $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\rho^{(k)} := (\boldsymbol{q}_k - \boldsymbol{p}_o)/\rho_k$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta^{(k)} \perp \hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\rho^{(k)}$. Under purely tangential motion ($u_\rho = 0$), Pythagoras applied to $\boldsymbol{q}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{q}_k + \Delta t\,\boldsymbol{u}_k$ gives
\begin{equation}
\rho_{k+1}^2 = \rho_k^2 + \Delta t^2 u_\theta^2,\quad
\delta_\text{chord} := \rho_{k+1}{-}\rho_k \approx \tfrac{\Delta t^2 u_\theta^2}{2\rho_k}.
\label{eq:m1}
\end{equation}
The discrete chord trajectory lies \emph{outside} the constant-$\rho$ arc, so single-step safety is preserved. The geometric cost is a rotation of the obstacle's bearing by
\begin{equation}
\phi_k = \arctan\!\bigl(\Delta t\, u_\theta / \rho_k\bigr) \approx \Delta t\, u_\theta / \rho_k,
\label{eq:m1-rot}
\end{equation}
which gives the world-frame velocity $\boldsymbol{u}_k$ a radial-inward component $\approx -\Delta t\, u_\theta^2 / \rho_k$ relative to the new radial direction $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\rho^{(k+1)}$. If the policy commits to this world-frame direction across steps (momentum at coarse filter rates), the inward drift compounds into M2.

M1 becomes operationally significant when $\Delta t \cdot \|u\| \gtrsim \sqrt{d_\text{safe} \cdot r}$. At $\Delta t = 0.1$s, $r = 0.2$m, $d_\text{safe} = 0.05$m, and $\|u\| = 1$m/s, the threshold is reached.

\subsubsection{M2: Multi-Step Drift from Sustained Commitment}

\textbf{Proposition 2} (Multi-step drift). Suppose the policy commits to a fixed world-frame velocity $\boldsymbol{u} = v\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta^{(0)}$ that was tangent at $\boldsymbol{q}_0$. By (\ref{eq:m1-rot}), the radial component of $\boldsymbol{u}$ relative to the bearing at step $k$ is $-v\sin\phi_k \approx -\Delta t\,v^2/\rho_k$, contributing per-step inward drift $\Delta t \cdot v\sin\phi_k \approx \Delta t^2 v^2/\rho_k$. Summing,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\rho_n := \rho_0 - \rho_n \;\le\; \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{\Delta t^2 v^2}{\rho_k}.
\label{eq:m2}
\end{equation}

Assuming $\rho_k \in [\rho_{\min}, \rho_{\max}]$ throughout, the drift accumulates \textbf{linearly} in $n$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{n\,\Delta t^2 v^2}{\rho_{\max}} \;\le\; \Delta\rho_n \;\le\; \frac{n\,\Delta t^2 v^2}{\rho_{\min}}.
\end{equation}

At $d_\text{safe} = 0.05$m, $\rho_{\min} = 0.25$m, $v = 1$m/s, $\Delta t = 0.1$s: per-step drift $\le 0.04$m, so $\Delta\rho_1 \le 0.04 < d_\text{safe}$ but $\Delta\rho_2 \le 0.08 > d_\text{safe}$ — penetration at $n^\star = 2$ steps. A single-step ($h{=}1$) filter cannot anticipate M2. Fig.~\ref{fig:v_a_trajectory} (left panel) visualises both effects: a static-constraint filter projects tangentially at the boundary but the M1 chord overshoot and M2 multi-step drift carry the agent through to $\rho_{\min}{=}0.076$\,m (collision); the right panel previews how the velocity augmentation (\S\ref{sec:method}) eliminates both.

\subsection{VA-ATACOM: The Velocity-Augmented Manifold}
\label{sec:design}

\textbf{From static to velocity-augmented constraint.} Both M1 and M2 arise because the static keepout constraint $\rho(\boldsymbol{q}) \ge r_\text{base}$ ignores the agent's momentum: at coarse $\Delta t$ the carried velocity cannot be cancelled in one step. We therefore augment the constraint with the agent's braking distance, $\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^2/(2a_{\max})$, where $a_{\max}$ is the maximum deceleration (calibrated once offline; \S\ref{sec:setup}).

\subsubsection{The Velocity-Augmented Constraint Manifold (core)}

Define the velocity-augmented barrier and its safe set
\begin{equation}
c(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{v}) = \rho(\boldsymbol{q}) - r_\text{base} - \frac{\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^2}{2a_{\max}} \ge 0,\quad r_\text{base} := r + d_\text{safe},
\label{eq:vam}
\end{equation}
whose zero-level set is a manifold in $(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{v})$ state space: the agent must reserve enough distance to brake to rest before the keepout band. With outward normal $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}} = (\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{p}_o)/\rho$ and acceleration command $\boldsymbol{a}$ (relative degree 2, $\dot{\boldsymbol{v}}=\boldsymbol{a}$), $\dot c = \hat{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{v} - (\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{a})/a_{\max}$; the exponential-CBF condition $\dot c \ge -\alpha_0 c$ becomes the \emph{linear} tangent-cone constraint
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{a} \;\le\; a_{\max}\bigl(\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{v} + \alpha_0\, c\bigr).
\label{eq:tangent_cone}
\end{equation}
VA-ATACOM projects the policy action onto this cone (a small QP; for the differential-drive Point robot the action maps as $\boldsymbol{a} = a_{\max}(\text{forward})\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta$, so only the forward component is constrained). At rest the constraint is vacuous; as $\boldsymbol{v}$ builds toward an obstacle it forces deceleration ($\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}<0$).

\textbf{Relation to the heuristic margin.} The widely-used velocity-adaptive margin $r_\text{eff}(\boldsymbol{v}) = r_\text{base}(1+\alpha\|\boldsymbol{v}\|)$ is the \emph{first-order} approximation of (\ref{eq:vam}): linearising the quadratic braking term gives an inflation $\propto \|\boldsymbol{v}\|$ with $\alpha \approx \|\boldsymbol{v}\|/(2a_{\max}r_\text{base})$. VA-ATACOM uses the exact quadratic constraint, which (\S\ref{sec:design-study}) is what lets the projection handle momentum.

\subsubsection{Auxiliary: BRT Reward Shaping (training aid for M2)}

We approximate the backward reachable tube by forward-rolling the point-mass dynamics under a $9$-direction discretisation $\mathcal{D}$ (eight evenly-spaced headings plus the current velocity direction) over $h$ steps, and record the deepest predicted keepout violation
\begin{equation}
V_\text{BRT}(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{v}) = \max_{d \in \mathcal{D}}\ \max_{1\le t\le h}\ \max_i\ \bigl(r - \|\boldsymbol{p}_t(d) - \boldsymbol{p}_o^{(i)}\|\bigr),
\label{eq:brt}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{p}_t(d)$ is the position after $t$ worst-case steps from $(\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{v})$. Rather than hard-trigger the filter, $V_\text{BRT}$ enters \emph{training} as a reward-shaping penalty
\begin{equation}
r_k \;\leftarrow\; r_k - \alpha_\text{BRT}\,\max\bigl(0,\,V_\text{BRT}(\boldsymbol{p}_k,\boldsymbol{v}_k)\bigr),
\notag
\end{equation}
biasing PPO away from states whose $h$-step worst-case rollout predicts penetration. The horizon is chosen to cover the penetration distance,
\begin{equation}
h \ge \left\lceil \frac{r}{\Delta t \cdot v_{\max}} \right\rceil,
\label{eq:h_cond}
\end{equation}
so any colliding direction is seen within the lookahead. Because Component~1 already enforces braking feasibility at every executed step, this lookahead is a \emph{soft} learning aid: it speeds convergence and damps the M2 goal-switching transients (supplementary material), but is not part of the Proposition~4 guarantee.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{VA-ATACOM safety filter (Component~1)}
\label{alg:dtatacom}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE policy action $\boldsymbol{a}_\text{RL}=(\text{fwd},\,\text{turn})$, pose $(\boldsymbol{p},\theta)$, velocity $\boldsymbol{v}$, obstacles $O$, params $(r_\text{base},\,a_{\max},\,\alpha_0,\,s)$
\FOR{each obstacle $o_i \in O$}
    \STATE $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_i \gets (\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_o^{(i)})/\|\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_o^{(i)}\|$
    \STATE $c_i \gets \|\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{p}_o^{(i)}\| - r_\text{base} - \|\boldsymbol{v}\|^2/(2a_{\max})$ \quad{\footnotesize// barrier (\ref{eq:vam})}
    \STATE $\text{rhs}_i \gets a_{\max}\,(\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_i\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{v} + \alpha_0\, c_i)$ \quad{\footnotesize// cone bound (\ref{eq:tangent_cone})}
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\text{rhs}_{\min} \gets \min_i \text{rhs}_i$ \quad{\footnotesize// bind all obstacles}
\STATE $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta \gets (\cos\theta,\,\sin\theta)$;\quad $\kappa \gets s\,(\boldsymbol{v}\!\cdot\!\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta)$ \quad{\footnotesize// diff-drive $\boldsymbol{a}{=}s\,\text{fwd}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta$}
\IF{$\kappa\cdot\text{fwd} \le \text{rhs}_{\min}$}
    \STATE \RETURN $\boldsymbol{a}_\text{RL}$ \quad{\footnotesize// cone constraint already satisfied}
\ENDIF
\STATE $\text{fwd}^\star \gets \text{rhs}_{\min}/\kappa$ \quad{\footnotesize// min-norm: only fwd changes}
\STATE $\text{fwd}_\text{safe} \gets
        \begin{cases}\min(\text{fwd},\text{fwd}^\star) & \kappa>0\\[-2pt]
                     \max(\text{fwd},\text{fwd}^\star) & \kappa<0\end{cases}$
\STATE $\text{fwd}_\text{safe} \gets \text{clip}(\text{fwd}_\text{safe},\,-1,\,1)$
\RETURN $(\text{fwd}_\text{safe},\,\text{turn})$ \quad{\footnotesize// turn left free}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Proposition 4: Discrete-Time Forward Invariance}
\label{sec:prop4}

\textbf{Theorem (Proposition 4).} Let $c_i(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{v}) = \rho_i(\boldsymbol{q}) - r_\text{base} - \|\boldsymbol{v}\|^2/(2a_{\max})$ be the velocity-augmented barriers (\ref{eq:vam}) of $m$ circular obstacles, with $\rho_i(\boldsymbol{q}) := \|\boldsymbol{q} - \boldsymbol{p}_o^{(i)}\|$, $r_\text{base} := r + d_\text{safe}$, and $c := \min_i c_i$. Consider the semi-implicit dynamics $\boldsymbol{v}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{v}_k + \Delta t\,\boldsymbol{a}_k$, $\boldsymbol{q}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{q}_k + \Delta t\,\boldsymbol{v}_{k+1}$ with $\|\boldsymbol{a}_k\| \le a_{\max}$ and $\|\boldsymbol{v}_k\| \le v_{\max}$, where $\boldsymbol{a}_k$ is the action returned by Algorithm~\ref{alg:dtatacom} (tangent-cone projection with rate $\alpha_0$). If
\begin{equation}
\alpha_0\Delta t \le 1,
\quad
d_\text{safe} \ge \frac{\tfrac{3}{2}\,\Delta t\,a_{\max}}{\alpha_0},
\quad
r \ge d_\text{safe} + \Delta t\,v_{\max},
\label{eq:prop4}
\end{equation}
then $c(\boldsymbol{q}_0,\boldsymbol{v}_0) \ge 0 \Rightarrow \rho_i(\boldsymbol{q}_k) \ge r$ for all obstacles $i$ and all $k \ge 0$: the executed trajectory is collision-free.

\textbf{Proof.} \emph{Step 1 (single-obstacle discrete-CBF recursion).} Fix an obstacle and write $\rho_k := \rho_i(\boldsymbol{q}_k)$, $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_k := (\boldsymbol{q}_k - \boldsymbol{p}_o^{(i)})/\rho_k$, $c_k := c_i(\boldsymbol{q}_k,\boldsymbol{v}_k)$. Squaring $\boldsymbol{q}_{k+1} - \boldsymbol{p}_o^{(i)} = (\boldsymbol{q}_k - \boldsymbol{p}_o^{(i)}) + \Delta t\,\boldsymbol{v}_{k+1}$ and using $\|\boldsymbol{v}_{k+1}\|^2 \ge (\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_k\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{v}_{k+1})^2$ gives the radial bound $\rho_{k+1} \ge \rho_k + \Delta t\,(\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_k\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{v}_{k+1})$ (valid as $\rho_k - \Delta t\,v_{\max} \ge 0$ under (\ref{eq:prop4})$_3$). Substituting $\boldsymbol{v}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{v}_k + \Delta t\,\boldsymbol{a}_k$ into $c_{k+1} = \rho_{k+1} - r_\text{base} - \|\boldsymbol{v}_{k+1}\|^2/(2a_{\max})$ and collecting orders of $\Delta t$,
\begin{equation}
c_{k+1} \ge c_k + \Delta t\Bigl[\underbrace{\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_k\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{v}_k - \tfrac{\boldsymbol{v}_k\cdot\boldsymbol{a}_k}{a_{\max}}}_{=\,\dot c_k}\Bigr] + \Delta t^2\Bigl[\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_k\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{a}_k - \tfrac{\|\boldsymbol{a}_k\|^2}{2a_{\max}}\Bigr].
\notag
\end{equation}
The tangent-cone constraint of Algorithm~\ref{alg:dtatacom}, $\boldsymbol{v}_k\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{a}_k \le a_{\max}(\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_k\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{v}_k + \alpha_0 c_k)$ (\ref{eq:tangent_cone}), makes the first bracket $\dot c_k \ge -\alpha_0 c_k$. The second bracket is minimised over $\|\boldsymbol{a}_k\| \le a_{\max}$ at $\boldsymbol{a}_k = -a_{\max}\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_k$, where it equals $-\tfrac{3}{2}a_{\max}$. Hence
\begin{equation}
c_{k+1} \;\ge\; (1 - \alpha_0\Delta t)\,c_k - \delta,
\qquad \delta := \tfrac{3}{2}\,\Delta t^2 a_{\max}.
\label{eq:dcbf-recursion}
\end{equation}
This certifies $c$ as a \emph{discrete-time control barrier function} with rate $\alpha_0$ and discretisation defect $\delta = O(\Delta t^2)$.

\emph{Step 2 (invariant level).} Since $0 \le 1 - \alpha_0\Delta t < 1$ by (\ref{eq:prop4})$_1$, the affine map $g(c) = (1-\alpha_0\Delta t)c - \delta$ is increasing with fixed point $c^\star = -\delta/(\alpha_0\Delta t) = -\tfrac{3}{2}\Delta t\,a_{\max}/\alpha_0$. As $c_0 \ge 0 > c^\star$ and (\ref{eq:dcbf-recursion}) gives $c_{k+1} \ge g(c_k)$, induction yields $c_k \ge c^\star$ for all $k$.

\emph{Step 3 (collision-free).} The filter binds the tightest obstacle, $\text{rhs}_{\min} = \min_i \text{rhs}_i$ (Alg.~\ref{alg:dtatacom}), so (\ref{eq:tangent_cone}) holds simultaneously for every obstacle and (\ref{eq:dcbf-recursion}) applies to each $c_i$. Thus $c_i(\boldsymbol{q}_k,\boldsymbol{v}_k) \ge c^\star$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
\rho_i(\boldsymbol{q}_k) \ge r_\text{base} + \tfrac{\|\boldsymbol{v}_k\|^2}{2a_{\max}} + c^\star \ge r_\text{base} - \tfrac{3}{2}\tfrac{\Delta t\,a_{\max}}{\alpha_0} \ge r,
\notag
\end{equation}
where the last step is (\ref{eq:prop4})$_2$. The separation condition (\ref{eq:prop4})$_3$, $r \ge d_\text{safe} + \Delta t\,v_{\max}$, guarantees the one-step displacement $\Delta t\,v_{\max}$ cannot carry the agent across a previously non-binding obstacle, so the binding index may switch between steps without breaking the bound. $\square$

\textbf{Remark (why the velocity term is essential).} The recursion (\ref{eq:dcbf-recursion}) is a discrete-time CBF on the \emph{velocity-augmented} barrier. DCM \cite{agrawal2017} imposes a constant-rate decay on the \emph{static} barrier $\rho - r_\text{base}$, which lacks the $\|\boldsymbol{v}\|^2/(2a_{\max})$ term and so cannot certify that the agent is able to brake; M1/M2 then violate its fixed-rate bound near the boundary (DCM: 3/15 GO). The braking term is exactly what converts the per-step inequality into a multi-step stopping guarantee.

\textbf{Remark (tightness).} The defect $\delta = \tfrac{3}{2}\Delta t^2 a_{\max}$ is a worst case attained only at $\boldsymbol{a}_k = -a_{\max}\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}_k$ (full acceleration straight into the obstacle). In the binding regime the cone constraint (\ref{eq:tangent_cone}) precludes this --- it would require $\boldsymbol{v}_k\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{a}_k$ above a near-zero right-hand side while approaching --- so the effective defect is dominated by the velocity-chord term and is much smaller. At Safety-Gym ($\Delta t{=}0.1$, $a_{\max}{=}0.9$, $\alpha_0{=}1$) the conservative bound is $d_\text{safe} \ge 0.135$, yet $d_\text{safe} = 0.05$ suffices empirically (\S\ref{sec:experiments}); (\ref{eq:prop4}) is thus sufficient with margin, not tight.

\textbf{Corollary 1 (Continuous-time limit).} As $\Delta t \to 0$ with $v_{\max}, a_{\max}, \alpha_0$ fixed, $\delta \to 0$ and the required $d_\text{safe} \to 0$: the tangent-cone projection (\ref{eq:tangent_cone}) becomes the continuous-time exponential-CBF condition $\dot c \ge -\alpha_0 c$ and VA-ATACOM reduces to the continuous-time tangent-projection flow $\dot{\boldsymbol{q}} = \boldsymbol{N}_c\boldsymbol{\alpha} - K_c\boldsymbol{J}_c^+\boldsymbol{c}$, recovering ATACOM's Theorem~1. The single-obstacle guarantee uses no multi-step lookahead: the braking term subsumes the horizon condition that the heuristic DT-margin (\S\ref{sec:design-study}) required, so the BRT (Component~2) is reward shaping, not part of this certificate.

\textbf{Corollary 2 (Multi-obstacle).} Alg.~\ref{alg:dtatacom}'s binding $\text{rhs}_{\min} = \min_i \text{rhs}_i$ makes (\ref{eq:tangent_cone}) hold for every $i$ simultaneously, so (\ref{eq:dcbf-recursion}) applies to each $c_i$ in parallel: $\rho_i(\boldsymbol q_k) \ge r$ for all $i,k$ at $O(m)$ per step.

\subsection{Constructive Hyperparameter Selection}

Given Safety-Gymnasium parameters ($\Delta t = 0.1$s, $r = 0.2$m, $d_\text{safe} = 0.05$m, $v_{\max} = 1.0$m/s, $a_{\max}=0.9$m/s$^2$), $r_\text{base} = 0.25$m. The braking term $\|v\|^2/(2a_{\max})$ reaches $0.67$m at top speed, and the BRT horizon (\ref{eq:h_cond}) prescribes $h \ge \lceil r/(\Delta t v_{\max})\rceil = 2$; we use $h=3$ (one step of margin) and exponential-CBF rate $\alpha_0=1$.

\subsection{Relation to Prior Work}

VA-ATACOM inherits ATACOM's null-space projection and adds the two discrete-time mechanisms; Corollary~1 shows it strictly generalises ATACOM as $\Delta t \to 0$. Unlike DCM \cite{agrawal2017}, which handles only single-step discrete CBF constraints, the multi-step BRT lookahead captures the M2 drift that single-step analysis misses (Prop.~2). Unlike offline HJ-reachability \cite{mitchell2005,bansal2017,fisac2019,hsu2021}, the BRT is a deterministic short-horizon online simulation, avoiding the curse of dimensionality and offline-to-online distribution shift at the cost of a horizon approximation.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Benchmark.} Safety-Gymnasium Point-Robot with three tasks: Goal, Push, MultiGoal.

\textbf{Metrics.} Episode cost $C$ (hazard entries per episode); GO threshold $\bar{C} \le 5.0$.

\textbf{Training.} 200K environment steps, 5 random seeds, PPO backbone.

\textbf{Calibration of $a_{\max}$.} The braking-distance constraint (\ref{eq:vam}) needs the agent's maximum deceleration. We measure it once offline: drive the Point robot to top speed, command full brake, and record $|\Delta\boldsymbol{v}|/\Delta t$. This gives $v_{\max}\approx1.13$\,m/s and $a_{\max}\approx0.9$\,m/s$^2$, so the braking distance at top speed is $\approx0.67$\,m --- $2.7\times$ the keepout radius, confirming momentum dominates at this control rate.

\textbf{Methods.} We compare eight published safety filters (ATACOM, ATACOM-VD/-S/-LA, HOCBF, DCM, Predictive ATACOM, CBF-QP), PPO-Lag, and an unfiltered PPO baseline against VA-ATACOM. To isolate the filter mechanism, every published filter is given our sim-BRT frontend (the same auxiliary reward-shaping aid), so any gap in Table~\ref{tab:main} is attributable to the filter itself.

\textbf{Hazard-radius matching.} Safety-Gymnasium uses $r{=}0.2$ on Goal/MultiGoal but $r{=}0.3$ on Push. For a fair comparison every filter that reads $r$ from its config (HOCBF, DCM, ATACOM-VD/-S/-LA, DT-margin, VA-ATACOM) is given the matching value per task; the few filters whose code does not parameterise $r$ (null-space ATACOM, Predictive ATACOM, CBF-QP) keep their built-in keepout distance ($d_{\text{safe}}{=}0.3$), which already matches Push. Earlier reports of these benchmarks used the Goal radius on Push, which under-protected the velocity-aware methods; the values in Table~\ref{tab:main} are the matched-radius numbers (10 methods $\times$ 5 seeds $\times$ 200K steps re-run for Push).

\section{Discussion}
%==============================================================================

\textbf{When to use VA-ATACOM vs ATACOM.} Use ATACOM when $\Delta t \cdot v_{\max} < \sqrt{d_\text{safe} \cdot r}$ (high-frequency control); use VA-ATACOM otherwise. At $r{=}0.2$m, $d_\text{safe}{=}0.05$m this is $\Delta t{=}30$ms: above it (Safety-Gymnasium, embedded controllers) VA-ATACOM is needed.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{L1: Conservative behaviour.} VA-ATACOM trades reward for safety via the inflated braking margin and BRT-shaping aid; in low-density scenes this can leave performance on the table relative to a tuned heuristic.

\textbf{L2: BRT-shaping basin near $h^\star \Delta t\,v_{\max} \approx r$.} When the lookahead horizon matches the penetration horizon (Safety-Gym $\Delta t{=}0.05$\,s), spurious shaping penalty traps training (supplementary material); a slower rate or softer BRT coefficient is needed.

\textbf{L3: Geometry assumptions.} Prop.~4 is stated for circular hazards and a disk-shaped agent. For convex hazards/agents, $\rho$ generalises to the signed inter-set distance with unchanged chord-defect bound; non-convex hazards need per-piece decomposition (Cor.~2's $O(m)$ then counts pieces).

\textbf{L4: Action-form coupling.} Our \texttt{diff\_drive} projection assumes forward speed and angular rate are independent; bicycle-like agents (e.g.\ Safety-Gym Car) require a steering-Jacobian variant. Cor.~2 still holds; details in the supplementary.

\subsection{Sim-to-Real Considerations}
\label{sec:sim2real}

Proposition~4's conditions are stated in physical units ($\Delta t$, $r$, $v_{\max}$, $d_\text{safe}$) and transfer to hardware once three deployment effects fold into the constants: \textbf{(i)} latency $\tau$ yields $\Delta t_\text{eff}{=}\Delta t{+}\tau$ in (\ref{eq:prop4}), pushing systems from ATACOM's regime into VA-ATACOM's; \textbf{(ii)} localisation noise $\sigma_\text{pos}$ inflates $r_\text{base}{\mapsto}r_\text{base}{+}k_\sigma\sigma_\text{pos}$; \textbf{(iii)} velocity tracking error means the separation condition should use a conservative $v_{\max}^\text{actual}$.

\textbf{(iv) Webots E-puck validation.} We port the unchanged VA-ATACOM filter to a Webots E-puck at $\Delta t{=}64$\,ms with raw noisy GPS ($\sigma{=}4$\,cm) and heading ($\sigma{=}0.05$\,rad), no state estimator. Across three layouts (corridor S-curve, dense field, L-barrier 90$^\circ$ turn; $3{\times}20{=}60$ trials each mode), VA-ATACOM yields $\mathbf{1/60}$ deep penetration of $r_\text{base}{=}0.135$\,m versus $\mathbf{14/60}$ without; goal-reach $\mathbf{60/60}$ with the filter vs $54/60$ without (6 trials stuck after collision), so the filter does not trade goal-reach for safety. \section{Conclusion}
%==============================================================================

VA-ATACOM proves \textbf{Proposition~4} --- the first discrete-time analogue of ATACOM's forward-invariance theorem on a velocity-augmented manifold. It reaches \textbf{15/15 GO} on Safety-Gymnasium ($p\!<\!0.001$) and reduces Webots E-puck deep penetrations $24\!\to\!1$ in $120$ paired trials ($p{=}1.2{\times}10^{-7}$).
