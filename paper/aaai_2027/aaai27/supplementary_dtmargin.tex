\section{The DT-margin Heuristic Baseline}
\label{app:dtmargin}

VA-ATACOM's velocity augmentation has a heuristic predecessor we call the \textbf{DT-margin} filter --- the ``DT-margin (heuristic)'' row of Table~1 in the main paper. It inflates the keepout radius linearly with speed, $r_\text{eff}(\boldsymbol{v}) = r_\text{base}(1+\alpha\|\boldsymbol{v}\|)$, projects out the inward radial component, and adds the same sim-BRT reward shaping. As shown in the main paper's design study, this linear margin is the \emph{first-order} approximation of VA-ATACOM's quadratic braking constraint; under fair hazard-radius matching it reaches 13/15 GO --- two Push seeds short of VA-ATACOM's 15/15, illustrating that the linearisation almost captures the right behaviour but leaves a residual gap that only the exact quadratic constraint closes. We characterise it here for completeness --- its two tunable knobs ($\alpha$ and the BRT horizon $h$) and their interaction with the control rate. These findings motivated, and are superseded by, the parameter-free velocity-augmented manifold of the main paper.

\label{sec:dt-sweep}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{DT-margin cost vs.\ time step ($\bar C \pm \sigma$, 5 seeds, 50K steps).}
\label{tab:dt}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
$\Delta t$ & ATACOM & DT-margin ($h{=}3$) & Note \\
\midrule

0.02s  & 0.00  & 0.00 & ATACOM OK \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:dt} corroborates the M1 chord-excursion premise of the main paper's failure-mode analysis (and hence Prop.~4): continuous-time ATACOM achieves zero cost at $\Delta t \le 0.02$s but fails at $\Delta t \ge 0.05$s, the phase transition matching the M1 threshold $\Delta t\|u\| \gtrsim \sqrt{d_\text{safe}\cdot r}$. The DT-margin heuristic tracks the safe regime at $\Delta t \in \{0.10, 0.20\}$s with $h{=}3$. At $\Delta t{=}0.05$s the picture is nuanced: the horizon rule prescribes $h^\star{=}4$, but a 5-seed sweep finds $h{=}3$ at 3/5 GO (bimodal), $h{=}4$ at 0/5, and $h{=}5$ at 3/5 (one seed at 37.05). We attribute this to a \emph{BRT-shaping basin}: near the penetration horizon the lookahead reports unavoidable penetration along multiple directions every step, integrating spurious penalty into the policy gradient and trapping training.

Only configurations at least one step above the minimum on \emph{both} axes ($\alpha{\ge}0.1$, $h{\ge}2$) reach 5/5 GO; setting $\alpha{=}0$ drops to 4/5 with a seed at $\bar C{=}8.46$. The DT-margin recipe is thus robust only with margin on both knobs; VA-ATACOM eliminates $\alpha$ entirely, replacing it with the calibrated $a_{\max}$.

A Friedman omnibus test across all 12 methods rejects equality with $\chi^2{=}97.05$, $p{=}6.8\!\times\!10^{-16}$ (df${=}11$).

\section{Generalisation Beyond Point-Robot Dynamics}
\label{sec:car-goal}

To probe VA-ATACOM's scope across different mobile-robot dynamics, we evaluate the unchanged filter on \texttt{SafetyCarGoal1-v0}, which replaces the Point agent's holonomic velocity actions with a bicycle-like throttle$+$steering. All other env parameters (8 hazards of radius 0.2, BRT frontend, 5 seeds, 200K steps) match the main Table~1.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{lrc}
\toprule
Method & mean $\bar C$ & GO/5\\
\midrule
PPO (no filter)        & 23.20 & 1/5\\
ATACOM (null-space)    & \textbf{9.20}  & 1/5\\
DT-margin (heuristic)  & 13.02 & 1/5\\
\textbf{VA-ATACOM}     & 23.92 & 1/5\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Every method is NO-GO under the 200K budget, but the relative ordering \emph{inverts} compared with the Point-robot Table~1: on Car, the null-space ATACOM (9.20) and DT-margin (13.02) beat VA-ATACOM (23.92).

The cause is structural and identified in our main paper: VA-ATACOM's tangent-cone projection is currently implemented with the \texttt{action\_form='diff\_drive'} constraint (\S\,VA-ATACOM Algorithm), which assumes \emph{forward speed and angular rate are independent actuators}. Car's bicycle model couples them through the wheelbase, so reducing forward command also reduces steerability --- the filter's safety brake removes the very degree of freedom the agent needs to turn away from the obstacle. The null-space ATACOM projection, by contrast, projects onto the Jacobian null-space of the position constraint, which is independent of the actuation parameterisation and degrades gracefully on Car.

This is a clear scope clarification for VA-ATACOM, not a refutation of Prop.~4 itself: the manifold and its discrete-time invariance theorem hold; the bicycle-Car case requires a different action-form projection (matching steering Jacobian) for the implementation. Designing a bicycle-aware action form is an obvious follow-up and is listed in the main paper's Limitations~L4.

\section{Anticipated Questions}
\label{sec:faq}

We pre-emptively address questions reviewers commonly raise on safety-filter papers.

\paragraph{Q1.\ Webots is not real hardware --- why claim "sim-to-real transfer"?}
We use the phrase narrowly. Safety-Gymnasium is a 2D point-mass abstraction; Webots is a 3D rigid-body simulator (Cyberbotics) with full ODE contact dynamics, a physical E-puck mesh (54.0\,mm diameter, 53.0\,mm wheel-base, accurate inertia and motor torque), and \emph{raw noisy GPS}/heading feeding the controller --- no state estimator. The transfer claim is therefore: from a holonomic point mass at 10\,Hz to a differential-drive rigid body at 16\,Hz with sensor noise, \emph{the unchanged filter} reduces deep penetrations 24$\rightarrow$1 in 120 paired trials. Physical-E-puck experiments using UWB localisation are listed as on-going work; the protocol is in our public repository.

\paragraph{Q2.\ Is VA-ATACOM just ATACOM + a velocity term?}
No. (i) The velocity augmentation alone is insufficient (Design row \emph{model-free braking scaling}, cost 117.4, 0/5 GO --- highest in Table~2); the tangent-cone projection onto the velocity-augmented manifold is what handles momentum. (ii) Proposition~4 is, to our knowledge, the first \emph{discrete-time} forward-invariance theorem for the ATACOM-style tangent projection, with an explicit chord-defect $\delta{=}\tfrac{3}{2}\Delta t^2 a_{\max}$ and constructive conditions on $(d_\text{safe}, \alpha_0)$ from $(\Delta t, r, v_{\max})$. (iii) Both ATACOM and the velocity-adaptive margin are recovered as limits/approximations (Cor.\,1, $\Delta t{\to}0$; first-order Taylor in $\|\boldsymbol v\|$).

\paragraph{Q3.\ DT-margin reaches 13/15 GO; VA-ATACOM 15/15. Is the 2-cell gain meaningful?}
Three arguments. (a) DT-margin requires hand-tuning $\alpha$ and a BRT horizon $h$ (see DT-margin sensitivity tables above); VA-ATACOM has zero hyperparameters once $a_{\max}$ is calibrated. (b) The Wilcoxon paired test rejects equality with $p{=}2.9{\times}10^{-3}$, and the 4 pairs that tie are exactly where both methods reach $\bar C{=}0$ --- in non-trivial cells DT-margin is strictly worse 10/11 times. (c) Theoretically, DT-margin is the first-order Taylor of VA-ATACOM in $\|\boldsymbol v\|$, so the residual gap is structural, not noise.

\paragraph{Q4.\ Are the Prop.~4 assumptions ($\alpha_0\Delta t{\le}1$, $d_\text{safe}{\ge}\tfrac{3}{2}\Delta t a_{\max}/\alpha_0$, $r{\ge}d_\text{safe}{+}\Delta t v_{\max}$) restrictive?}
They are constructive, not restrictive. At Safety-Gym defaults ($\Delta t{=}0.1$\,s, $v_{\max}{=}1$\,m/s, $a_{\max}{=}0.9$\,m/s$^2$, $\alpha_0{=}1$), the bounds give $d_\text{safe}{\ge}0.135$\,m and $r{\ge}0.235$\,m, both met by the env defaults ($r{=}0.2$/$0.3$). The first two come from the discretisation defect $\delta$ in Step~2 of the proof; the third is a steady-state distance budget. All three are checked at run-time and printed in our logs.

\paragraph{Q5.\ Why no GPU? Would a larger PPO net change the picture?}
The bottleneck is environment simulation, not the policy (60-dim obs, 64-64 MLP). On a 28-thread CPU we run 20 parallel 200K trainings in 10\,h wall-clock; a GPU yields a small speed-up but the failure modes (M1/M2) are about the constraint, not the model class. We trained PPO-Lag (the only filter-free safe-RL method in our sweep) on the same 64-64 MLP for fair comparison; it reaches 1/15 GO.

\section{Detailed Comparison with Related Filters}
\label{sec:related-detail}

The table below isolates VA-ATACOM from the five closest tangent-projection / barrier filters along six axes. Empirical GO rows are from the main paper's Table~1 (Safety-Gymnasium, $5$ seeds $\times$ $200$K, fair hazard-radius match).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Axis & ATACOM & HOCBF & DCBF & DT-marg. & \textbf{VA-ATACOM}\\
\midrule
Time domain & cont. & cont. & disc. & disc. & \textbf{disc.}\\
Constraint  & tangent & class-$\kappa$ & static $\gamma$ & vel.\,inflation & \textbf{vel.-aug.\ manifold}\\
Defect bound & — & tune & const.\,$\gamma$ & none & $\boldsymbol{\tfrac{3}{2}\Delta t^2 a_{\max}}$\\
Hyper-params & gains & class-$\kappa$ & $\gamma$ & $\alpha,h$ & \textbf{0 post-$a_{\max}$ calib}\\
Multi-obs. cost & per-step & per-step & per-step & per-step & $\boldsymbol{O(m)}$ (Cor.~2)\\
\midrule
Empirical GO/15 & 0/15 & 4/15 & 3/15 & 13/15 & \textbf{15/15}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

VA-ATACOM dominates on four axes(discrete-time domain $+$ explicit defect bound $+$ zero post-calibration hyperparameters $+$ multi-obstacle complexity $+$ empirical GO) while strictly subsuming the other four(Cor.~1 recovers ATACOM as $\Delta t{\to}0$;DT-margin is its first-order Taylor in $\|\boldsymbol v\|$). HOCBF and DCBF remain orthogonal extensions(higher relative-degree and discrete-time static barrier respectively),neither of which addresses the velocity-augmented projection nor the explicit discrete-time invariance defect that Prop.~4 supplies.

\section{Computing Infrastructure}
\label{sec:infra}

All experiments reported in the main paper and this supplementary were run on a single workstation:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{CPU}: Intel Core i7-13850HX (20 cores, 28 threads).
\item \textbf{RAM}: 31\,GiB DDR5.
\item \textbf{OS}: Ubuntu 24.04 LTS, Linux kernel 6.17.
\item \textbf{GPU}: not used --- all training and evaluation run \emph{single-threaded on CPU} (\texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS=1}, \texttt{MKL\_NUM\_THREADS=1}). Each Safety-Gymnasium 200K-step run takes $\approx$\,15--20\,min wall-clock; we parallelise up to 20 such single-threaded runs at once.
\end{itemize}

Software stack (versions used at experiment time):

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item Python 3.10
\item PyTorch 2.x (CPU build)
\item NumPy 1.23 / matplotlib 3.x
\item \texttt{safety-gymnasium} 1.0 (the Safety-Gym successor; we ran with \texttt{SafetyPointGoal1-v0}, \texttt{SafetyPointPush1-v0}, \texttt{SafetyPointMultiGoal0-v0})
\item \texttt{gymnasium} 0.28 / \texttt{mujoco} 2.3
\item Webots R2023b (for the rigid-body validation in main \S\,Sim-to-Real)
\end{itemize}

Random seeds 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 are used for every (method, task) cell in Table~1 of the main paper. Total compute for the full Table~1 + Webots study is approximately \textbf{200 CPU-hours} on the workstation above ($\sim$\,10\,h wall-clock with 20-way parallelism); the L-shape audit re-run added a further $\sim$\,5 CPU-hours.
